## Script

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "Library of Alexandria illustration" — the tested
public-domain O. Von Corven engraving, `alexandria_library.jpg`]**

**HOOK (0:00-0:15):** Before you believe the caliph burned the Library of Alexandria,
know the story has zero evidence from the century it happened in.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "ancient Alexandria fire illustration"]**

The real story isn't one fire — it's a three-hundred-year death with at least three
different suspects, and the most famous one probably never touched a single scroll.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "ancient Library of Alexandria depiction"]**

**THE TURN (0:15-0:30):** Today we're tracing what actually happened to the Library
of Alexandria — what's solid, what historians still argue about, and the one popular
version that's almost certainly a myth.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "Ptolemy I Soter bust" / "ancient Alexandria map"]**

**BEAT 1 — The founding (SOLID):** The Library was part of the Mouseion, a research
institution founded in the early Ptolemaic dynasty, in Egypt.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "Demetrius of Phalerum"]**

Tradition credits Demetrius of Phalerum with proposing it to Ptolemy I — though some
historians call his exact role debatable, so treat that detail as likely, not certain.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "ancient papyrus scroll"]**

**BEAT 2 — How big was it, really (SOLID, with a caveat):** Nobody knows exactly how
many scrolls it held.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "ancient library scrolls shelves"]**

Estimates range from 40,000 to as high as 700,000 — but that top figure comes from
ancient writers centuries later, and modern historians consider it almost certainly
exaggerated.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "Julius Caesar bust" / "ancient Alexandria harbor"]**

**BEAT 3 — Caesar's fire, 48 BCE (CONTESTED):** The most commonly blamed culprit.
Caesar's own account only mentions burning ships and docks —

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "ancient scribe writing scroll"]**

it's a later writer, Strabo, writing twenty-five years afterward, who connects the
fire to the library.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "ancient Alexandria library ruins"]**

Historians still dispute how much was actually lost, and there's evidence the library
kept functioning afterward.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "Serapeum Alexandria ruins" — or AI-generate: "4th
century CE temple destruction, Alexandria, dramatic lighting, historical illustration
style" if no real image is found]**

**BEAT 4 — The Serapeum, 391 CE (SOLID temple destruction / UNCERTAIN library link):**
In 391 CE, the Serapeum temple in Alexandria was destroyed under Bishop Theophilus —
that part is well documented by near-contemporary writers.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "abandoned ancient ruins"]**

What's *not* documented: any ancient source actually connecting that destruction to a
library collection still existing at the time.

**[ON SCREEN: AI-generate: "ancient temple ruins overlaid with faded library scrolls,
conceptual historical illustration, muted tones"]**

The two events get merged in popular retellings. The sources don't support that merge.

**[ON SCREEN: AI-generate: "medieval manuscript illustration style, books and scrolls,
myth and legend visual framing"]**

**BEAT 5 — The caliph myth, 641 CE (UNCERTAIN — treat as legend):** The most famous
version — a caliph ordering the library burned because its books were either
redundant with or contrary to the Quran —

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "medieval scribe illustration"]**

traces to a single writer, Bar Hebraeus, writing roughly six hundred years after the
fact.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "ancient empty archive shelves"]**

No source from the century it supposedly happened mentions it.

**[ON SCREEN: AI-generate: "two identical ancient libraries side by side, mirrored
illustration, myth and legend visual framing"]**

And the same exact story gets told about a different library, under the same caliph,
somewhere else entirely — a strong sign it's a reused legend, not a record.

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "ancient Alexandria ruins present day"]**

**PAYOFF (delivering the hook's promise):** So the Library of Alexandria wasn't
destroyed. It was worn down — a fire here, a purge there, a temple demolition that
got conflated with it later, across three centuries —

**[ON SCREEN: Wikimedia Commons: "ancient Alexandria harbor sunset"]**

and the single-villain version everyone repeats is the one part of the story with the
least evidence behind it.
